Invited talks

  1. Nov 2025
    Invited seminar

    Towards secure artificial intelligence: private distributed learning and strategic decision making

    Institute of Information Theory and Automation (UTIA), Czech Academy of Sciences · Prague, Czech Republic

    Seminar page
    Abstract
    Artificial intelligence faces security challenges at many levels, such as the exposure of sensitive data, the vulnerability of distributed learning systems, and the need to design robust policies under adversarial uncertainty. This seminar discussed two approaches to improve AI security: federated learning to preserve privacy across distributed data sources, and Adversarial Risk Analysis (ARA) to formulate decision-theoretic defensive planning against stochastic adversarial agents.

  3. Jun 2025
    Oral

    Adversarial risk analysis with fully probabilistic designs to handle sequential games

    EURO 2025 Congress · Leeds, United Kingdom

    Abstract
    Adversarial risk analysis (ARA) is a decision-analytic methodology that informs decision making when facing intelligent opponents and uncertain outcomes. It enables an analyst to model beliefs about an opponent's utilities, capabilities, probabilities and strategic calculations. This talk discussed how to merge ARA and Fully Probabilistic Design to handle sequential games.
  4. Jun 2025
    Oral

    Adversarial risk analysis with fully probabilistic designs

    XLI National Congress of Statistics and Operations Research (SEIO 2025) · Lleida, Spain

    Abstract
    ARA applied to defend–attack games mitigates the standard common-knowledge and common-prior assumptions of classical game theory, yet it entails complicated modelling and computation. This work discussed a new approach to ARA using Fully Probabilistic Design in simple defend–attack games.

  6. Jul 2024
    Poster

    Delayed spiking neural networks for neuromorphic computing

    AIHUB Connexion Summer School · Valencia, Spain

    Poster
    Abstract
    Spiking Neural Networks compute with discrete spikes instead of continuous activations. Adding learnable synaptic delays improves their temporal learning while reducing the number of parameters. This poster presented an empirical study of delay-augmented SNNs on neuromorphic spatio-temporal datasets.
