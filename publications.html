Adversarial risk analysis with fully probabilistic designs In press

Mario Chacón-Falcón, Tatiana Valentine Guy, Miroslav Kárný, David Ríos Insua

Reliability Engineering & System Safety (journal version in production) · preprint arXiv:2611.14289

arXiv PDF
Abstract
Adversarial Risk Analysis (ARA) supports decision makers facing intelligent opponents and uncertain outcomes by modelling the opponents' preferences, capabilities, beliefs and strategic reasoning, but it entails complex modelling and computations. We combine ARA with Fully Probabilistic Design (FPD), a control-theoretic approach that selects the decision distribution whose induced outcome distribution is closest, in Kullback–Leibler divergence, to an ideal one, and use it to model and forecast the opponent's decisions. We show how to handle single- and multi-stage security games within this ARA–FPD framework, with applications to the reliability and risk analysis of critical infrastructure.

Privacy-preserving federated learning AdaBoost via knowledge distillation

Mario Chacón-Falcón, Jose M. Moyano

Knowledge-Based Systems, vol. 339, article 116962, 2026

Paper Code Supplementary
Abstract
Federated Learning (FL) provides a decentralized machine learning paradigm allowing multiple clients to collaboratively train models without sharing raw data. However, data heterogeneity across clients and privacy risks during model exchanges remain critical challenges. In this paper, we propose a novel privacy-preserving adaptation of AdaBoost for Federated Learning that leverages knowledge distillation to effectively mitigate non-IID data skew while preventing sensitive information leakage during aggregation, enhancing both model accuracy and privacy guarantees.

BAM-SLDK: Biologically inspired attention mechanism with spiking learnable delayed kernel synapses

Mario Chacón-Falcón, Alberto Patiño-Saucedo, Luis Camuñas-Mesa, Teresa Serrano-Gotarredona, Bernabé Linares-Barranco

Neuromorphic Computing and Engineering (IOP), vol. 5, no. 1, 2025

Paper Code Visual demo
Abstract
Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) are emerging as an alternative neural network model due to their biological plausibility, energy efficiency, and built-in ability to learn from temporal dynamics. However, in order to effectively process data with rich spatial and temporal dependencies, the usual static projections (feedforward and recurrent) among layers of spiking neurons fail to represent all the information needed. Inspired by how synaptic delays affect the learning process in biological neurons, we propose a biologically inspired attention mechanism based on spiking convolutions with learnable delayed kernel synapses. The proposed model increases temporal learning ability, attending simultaneously to spatial and temporal dynamics with few parameters required.
