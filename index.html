Mario Chacón-Falcón

PhD candidate in Mathematics · ICMAT-CSIC & Universidad Autónoma de Madrid

I study how learning agents can make good decisions when the world, and the other agents in it, are uncertain and changing. My research sits between reinforcement learning and Bayesian decision theory: adaptive agents that infer latent goals and shifting dynamics, multi-agent RL with explicit beliefs about opponents, and adversarial risk analysis for sequential strategic problems.

My PhD is funded by the Momentum CSIC programme. Before my PhD I completed an M.S. in Logic, Computation and AI and a B.S. in Mathematics at the Universidad de Sevilla, and worked on spiking neural networks at IMSE-CNM.

Research interests

Adaptive RL

Agents that track latent goals and non-stationary dynamics, and adapt their policy as their beliefs about the task change.

Multi-agent RL

Learning against and alongside other strategic agents: opponent modelling, robustness to exploitation.

Bayesian decision making

Adversarial risk analysis: designing agents with principled beliefs about opponents.

Robust & secure learning

Robust policies for high-stakes settings and privacy-preserving distributed learning.
